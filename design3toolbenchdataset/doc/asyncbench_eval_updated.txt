\subsubsection{Asynchronous Scheduling Evaluation}
The full balanced AsyncBench split contains 35,654 valid asynchronous episodes derived from ToolBench.
Using the same Llama-3.2-1B-Instruct tokenizer as our benchmark, the average linearized transcript length is 1098.6 tokens, while the average benchmark prefill context length is 870.8 tokens; the corresponding maxima are 4249 and 3846 tokens, respectively.
For a more stable runtime comparison, we report results on a fixed 300-sample subset drawn from the full split with seed 42.

We evaluate four real append-only online schedules and two canonical-order controls on AsyncBench:
(1)~\emph{Synchronous} waits for all tool returns before prefilling the full context;
(2)~\emph{Chunked} prefills each contiguous logical prefix as it becomes available;
(3)~\emph{Link-Last} prefills each tool block on arrival but defers all Link fragments to a final tail pass;
(4)~\emph{LinkCLAW} (LAP) also prefills blocks on arrival and greedily appends each newly unlocked Link chain in the same incremental pass;
(5)~\emph{Link-Last-C} keeps the same online block schedule as Link-Last but evaluates a canonical block-link-block-link prefix assembled with explicit positions and multi-cache replay; and
(6)~\emph{LinkCLAW-C} is the analogous canonical-order control for LinkCLAW.
Because whole-answer strict EM remains 0.0 for all six schedules on the current checkpoint, Table~\ref{tab:asyncbench_overlap} focuses on \emph{Decomp. Acc.} together with overlap-aware latency metrics. We omit LLM-judge scores here and reserve judge-based evaluation for the smaller judged subset.

Table~\ref{tab:asyncbench_overlap} shows two distinct effects.
First, Stream and Chunked achieve identical Decomp. Acc. (23.3\%), confirming that simple asynchronous chunking does not hurt answer quality when the final logical prefix is preserved.
Second, among the real online schedules, LinkCLAW retains the strongest systems behavior: relative to Link-Last, it cuts residual tail prefill from 31.8\,ms to 15.9\,ms (50.0\% reduction) and raises append-overlap from 48.7\% to 64.9\%, while changing semantic accuracy only slightly (22.3\% to 22.7\%).

The canonical controls clarify where the online accuracy gap comes from.
Once the final scored prefix is normalized to the same block-link-block-link order, Link-Last-C and LinkCLAW-C recover identical Decomp. Acc. (23.3\%), matching Stream and Chunked.
This indicates that the small semantic difference between the real online Link-Last and LinkCLAW schedules is largely a prefix-order effect rather than an intrinsic advantage of one Link policy over the other.
At the same time, LinkCLAW-C still preserves the better systems signature of LinkCLAW, with lower post-tool latency (3368.2\,ms vs. 3413.0\,ms) and much higher overlap (64.8\% vs. 48.6\%).

Overall, these results support a clean separation of concerns.
Canonical-order controls show that Link-Last and LinkCLAW converge in answer quality once the scored prefix is held fixed, whereas the real online comparison shows that LinkCLAW remains the best append-only schedule for removing the prefill scheduling bubble during asynchronous multi-tool execution.
